1 Module overview

Overview

COMP1860 - Building our Digital World: Computer Systems & Architecture introduces the fundamental principles and practices underlying modern computer systems. As a core com ponent of the Computer Science curriculum, the module develops students’ understanding of how hardware and software components work together to process data, execute programs and provide computing services. It combines theoretical concepts with practical applications, with particular emphasis on computer architecture, networking, operating systems and the engineer ing considerations involved in the design of computer systems.

The module covers essential concepts in computer systems, including digital systems, data representation, computer architecture and assembly-level machine organisation. Students are introduced to common development platforms, input/output fundamentals, interactive systems and the organisation of computer networks. The module also explores modern architectural concepts such as pipelining, scaling, caching, multi-core and heterogeneous architectures, along side fundamental principles of operating systems.

Through a combination of theoretical teaching and practical applications, students develop the ability to design, assemble, troubleshoot and evaluate computer systems. Particular emphasis is placed on applying engineering design principles and selecting appropriate tools to design, test, analyse and evaluate systems. Students also consider system requirements and the defini tion, measurement and evaluation of performance metrics, developing an understanding of the trade-offs involved in designing reliable and effective computer systems.

The module also considers the wider responsibilities associated with computer systems engi neering. Students are introduced to fundamental security considerations and the identification and mitigation of security risks, as well as ethical concerns arising from the design and im plementation of computer systems. Communication, teamwork, time management, problem solving, critical thinking, active learning and reflection are developed alongside technical sys tems knowledge.

COMP1860 provides an important foundation for subsequent study in computer science and related areas of computing. By the end of the module, students should be able to apply computer systems and engineering principles to specified requirements, use appropriate tools to analyse and evaluate systems, identify security and ethical considerations, communicate complex technical ideas to both technical and non-technical audiences, and reflect on their developing knowledge and skills.

Aims

The aim of COMP1860 is to develop students’ understanding of the fundamental principles of computer systems and architecture, and their ability to apply these principles to the design, implementation, testing and evaluation of computing systems. The module develops an en gineering perspective on system design, considering performance, reliability, security, risk and ethical considerations, while equipping students with the technical and problem-solving skills required to analyse and communicate about modern computer systems.

Rationale

COMP1860 provides students with an understanding of the computer systems and architec tures that underpin modern computing, giving them knowledge that is directly applicable to professional practice. In industry, computing professionals rarely work with software in iso lation; applications run on processors, interact with memory and storage, communicate over networks, and depend on operating systems and other system components. Understanding these interactions enables graduates to reason more effectively about how their software and systems behave in real-world environments.

Within the Computer Science degree, COMP1860 complements programming and theoretical study by providing the systems perspective needed to understand how computational concepts are implemented and used in practice. The module provides a foundation for more advanced study in areas such as operating systems, networking, cybersecurity, distributed and parallel computing, cloud computing, embedded systems and software engineering. It also develops an understanding of system-level trade-offs, including performance, reliability, security and re source utilisation, which are important when designing and evaluating computing solutions.

The knowledge and skills developed in COMP1860 contribute directly to students’ future ca reers by enabling them to design, develop and evaluate software with a better understanding of the environment in which it will operate. For software developers and software engineers, this can support more effective debugging, performance analysis and system design. For pro fessionals working in systems, cloud or infrastructure roles, it provides essential knowledge of computer architecture, operating systems and networking. For those entering cybersecurity, it establishes an understanding of the systems and architectures that must be protected. More broadly, the module develops the ability to analyse complex systems, identify constraints and risks, select appropriate tools, and make informed engineering decisions. These capabilities are transferable across a wide range of computing roles and provide a foundation for adapting to new technologies and taking on increasingly complex technical responsibilities throughout a graduate’s career.

Learning outcomes

On successful completion of this module a student will have demonstrated the ability to:

  1. Describe and apply subject specific knowledge and engineering design principles to design and implement computer systems considering well specified requirements (C1, M1, C2, M2, C5, M5, C6, M6)
  2. Use appropriately selected tools to design, test, analyse and evaluate computer systems and identify limitations (C12, M12, C13, M13)
  3. Select and interpret sources of information to solve complex real-world problems. (C4, M4)
  4. Identify and interpret risk concerning security and plan a mitigation approach (C10, M10)
  5. Identify and analyse ethical concerns when designing and implementing computer systems and make reasoned decisions informed by ethical frameworks and codes of conduct. (C8, M8)
  6. Effectively communicate complex topics concerning computer systems to technical and non-technical audiences. (C17, M17)
  7. Reflect on their level of mastery of subject knowledge and skills and plan for personal development. (C18, M18)

2 Logistics Teaching approach

The module has been designed to be interactive and engaging. All instructor led activities are on campus and attendance is expected. During instructor led sessions you will be engaged in learning through direct delivery from a member of teaching staff in a lecture theatre, com puter lab or tutorial room. During these sessions you may also be asked to complete exercises, work in small groups, discuss a topic, contribute to a discussion, engage in online polling and otherwise make contributions to the session. If you have reasonable adjustments as part of a disability support summary then please make yourself known to the member of staff delivering the session. The active approach to learning employed by this module means you will get the most out of the instructor led sessions by engaging in all activities, if you are unsure about the activity then please ask the instructor.

Each week you will be given an activity sheet which contains activities for you to complete during your independent study time. Some of the exercises on the activity sheet form part of your portfolio of evidence and should be submitted via Minerva. During lab classes and tutorials the activity sheet will be discussed. Sample solutions will be provided for all formative exercises on the activity sheets.

Lab and tutorial classes

Lab and tutorial classes provide an opportunity to apply the concepts and techniques introduced during the instructor led sessions. These sessions are designed to be practical and participatory, giving you time to work through problems, complete exercises and develop your understanding with support from the teaching team.

During lab classes you can expect to work through practical activities, exercises and examples, often using relevant software, tools or other resources. You may be asked to work individually or collaboratively with other students. Teaching staff will be available to provide guidance, answer questions, clarify concepts and help you identify and address areas where you may need further development. You should come prepared to participate actively and to discuss your approach to the activities with the teaching team and your peers.

Tutorial classes provide an opportunity to explore topics in greater depth and to develop your ability to apply your knowledge to different problems and scenarios. You may be asked to discuss questions, explain your reasoning, work through problems, review your answers or contribute to group activities. Tutorials are also an opportunity to receive feedback on your understanding and to identify areas for further independent study.

You should use both lab and tutorial classes as opportunities to ask questions and test your understanding. You are not expected to know how to complete every activity before attending; making mistakes and discussing different approaches are important parts of the learning pro cess. To get the most from these sessions, you should review the relevant material beforehand, attempt the activities on the weekly activity sheet and actively engage with the tasks during the session.

Skills development

As well as developing your subject knowledge, this module will support the development of a range of academic, practical and transferable skills. The activities undertaken during instructor led sessions, labs, tutorials and independent study are designed to give you opportunities to practise these skills and apply them in different contexts.

You will develop your ability to analyse problems, select appropriate approaches and apply your knowledge to reach and justify solutions. You will also develop your ability to work indepen dently, manage your time and take responsibility for your own learning through the completion of weekly activities and portfolio evidence.

Where activities involve working with other students, you will have opportunities to develop communication, collaboration and teamwork skills. You may also be required to explain your ideas, discuss alternative approaches and provide or respond to feedback. These activities are intended to help you become more confident in communicating technical or subject-specific information to different audiences.

The module will also provide opportunities to develop practical and digital skills relevant to the subject. Through lab activities and other practical exercises, you will gain experience of using appropriate tools, techniques and resources, while developing the ability to evaluate your own work and identify areas for improvement.

You should view feedback from teaching staff, sample solutions and your own reflection on completed activities as important parts of your skills development. Regularly reviewing your work and acting on feedback will help you to develop greater confidence, independence and proficiency throughout the module.

3 Attendance & Engagement expectations Attendance expectations

You are expected to attend all timetabled sessions associated with your modules and pro gramme. This includes lectures, tutorials, practical sessions, laboratories, workshops, seminars, group activities, and any other scheduled teaching or learning activities.

Regular attendance is an important part of your successful study. Attending timetabled sessions gives you opportunities to develop your subject knowledge and practical skills, ask questions, receive feedback, and work through material with teaching staff and other students. For a computer science degree, attendance is particularly important for practical and collaborative activities, where you develop your learning through hands-on work, problem solving, and in teraction with others.

Attendance also contributes to your wider student experience. Being present on campus gives you opportunities to build relationships with other students, develop a sense of belonging to your programme and the University community, and participate in academic and social activi ties. Regular attendance can also help staff identify when you may be experiencing difficulties and provide appropriate support at an early stage. Attendance is therefore not simply an aca demic expectation; it is also an important part of your wellbeing and of making the most of your university experience.

If you are unable to attend a timetabled session, for example because of illness or other cir cumstances affecting your ability to study, you should follow the University’s procedures for reporting an absence and, where appropriate, seek advice and support.

Participation requirements

You are expected to actively participate in all scheduled learning activities and to engage con sistently with your studies more generally. Attendance is an important starting point, but being present is not in itself sufficient: you should take an active role in your learning.

Active participation may include preparing for sessions, completing assigned activities, con tributing to discussions, attempting practical exercises, working constructively with your peers, asking questions, seeking and responding to feedback, and completing independent study and assessment activities by the required deadlines.

You are also expected to engage with the wider University experience and to take reasonable responsibility for your own academic progress and development. This includes making effective use of the learning resources, support services, academic guidance, and opportunities available to you.

Your university experience is an active process, and you generally get out of your degree what you put into it. Consistent engagement will help you develop not only your technical and aca demic knowledge, but also the communication, collaboration, problem-solving, and professional skills that are important for a career in computer science.

Conduct in lectures, tutorials and laboratories

You are expected to conduct yourself appropriately in all teaching sessions and to contribute to a positive, inclusive, and productive learning environment. Your behaviour affects not only your own learning, but also the ability of other students and teaching staff to participate effec tively.

You should arrive punctually and be ready to participate at the scheduled start time. Arriving late can disrupt the session and distract other students, so you should make every reasonable effort to arrive on time. If you do need to arrive late, you should do so as quietly and unobtru sively as possible.

You are expected to treat your fellow students, teaching staff, technical staff, and other mem bers of the University community with courtesy and respect. You should listen when others are speaking, allow others to contribute, and respect that your classmates may have different backgrounds, experiences, perspectives, and levels of prior knowledge. Behaviour that intimi dates, disrupts, excludes, or prevents others from learning is not acceptable.

You are expected to engage actively and constructively with teaching and learning activities. This includes listening and contributing during lectures, participating in discussions and activ ities during tutorials, and working safely and collaboratively during laboratory sessions. You should ask questions when you need clarification and should be willing to consider and respond to the ideas and contributions of others. In group activities, you are expected to contribute fairly and to support a constructive working environment.

You may use laptops, tablets, phones, and other technology during teaching sessions where this supports your learning or is required for the activity. However, your use of technology should not distract you or others from learning. You should not use devices for activities that are unrelated to the session where doing so is disruptive or prevents you from participating effec tively. Teaching staff may provide specific instructions about when and how devices should be used during particular sessions, and you are expected to follow these instructions.

In laboratory sessions, you must follow all instructions relating to health and safety, the use of equipment and computing facilities, and the appropriate use of University systems. You should take reasonable care of the facilities and equipment provided to you and report any problems or damage promptly. You must not undertake activities that could compromise the security, integrity, or availability of University computing systems or interfere with the work of other students.

You should also be mindful of the learning environment when taking breaks, leaving a session, or returning to it. Where you need to leave a session temporarily, do so with as little disruption as possible.

Ultimately, you are responsible for contributing to an environment in which everyone can learn effectively. Professional and respectful conduct is an important part of studying computer sci ence and will help you develop the behaviours and collaborative skills expected in academic and professional environments.

Teaching staff may ask you to adjust your conduct if they consider that it is affecting the learning environment. In more serious or persistent cases, you may be asked to leave the session. You are expected to follow such instructions promptly and respectfully. Any concerns about the instruction should be raised with the appropriate member of staff after the session,

rather than disrupting teaching.

Monitoring engagement

The University actively monitors your attendance and engagement. Your attendance at timetabled activities is recorded through the University’s attendance monitoring system. Further informa tion about attendance monitoring, including how attendance is recorded and what you should do if you are unable to attend, is available on the University’s website:

https://students.leeds.ac.uk/attendance-absences

Attendance monitoring is intended to support you as well as to identify patterns of non attendance. If you are regularly absent or your engagement gives cause for concern, the University or programme team may contact you to discuss your circumstances and identify whether you need any additional support.

You should therefore not regard attendance monitoring simply as an administrative require ment. It provides an opportunity for the University to identify when you may be experiencing academic, personal, health, financial, or other difficulties and to help you access appropriate sources of support.

Consequences of poor attendance

Regular non-attendance can have a significant impact on your academic progress and your wider university experience. If you do not attend teaching activities, you may miss essential explanations, practical work, opportunities to ask questions, feedback, and collaborative learn ing with your peers. Persistent non-attendance can therefore make it more difficult for you to meet the learning outcomes of your modules and succeed in your assessments.

If you have a pattern of poor attendance or engagement, the programme team may contact you to discuss your circumstances and your plans for re-engaging with your studies. Where ap propriate, you may be referred to relevant University support services or asked to take specific steps to improve your attendance and engagement.

You should be aware that persistent non-attendance may also have consequences under Uni versity regulations and procedures, including where attendance or engagement is a requirement for particular modules, placements, professional activities, or continued registration on your programme. If you are unable to attend regularly because of circumstances affecting your stud ies, you should seek advice at the earliest opportunity rather than allowing non-attendance to continue without explanation.

The purpose of responding to poor attendance is not simply punitive. Early intervention allows the University to understand the reasons for your non-attendance, provide appropriate support, and help you re-engage with your studies wherever possible. If you are experiencing difficulties that are affecting your attendance or engagement, you are encouraged to communicate with your academic or personal tutor and make use of the University’s support services.

5 Assessment & Feedback

Assessments structure

There are two types of assessment used in this module, formative and summative assessment. Formative assessment is a type of evaluation used in education to monitor student learning and provide ongoing feedback that can be used by instructors and students to improve teaching and learning. Formative assessments will be used by the lab and tutorial leaders to assess your understanding and to guide your learning. Summative assessment is a type of evaluation used to measure student learning, knowledge, proficiency, or achievement at the end of an instruc tional period. This type of assessment is designed to determine if the learning outcomes and standards of the curriculum have been met. Summative assessment will be used through the module via the portfolio and at the end of the teaching period by terminal assessment.

This module assessment has two components, you will be given an opportunity to attempt both components. The two components are a portfolio and terminal assessments. You are require to submit and pass the portfolio in order to be awarded credit for this module.

Portfolio

The portfolio is a collection of exercises which are submitted via Minerva throughout the aca demic year. Each week you will receive a worksheet which will clearly identify which exercises are part of the portfolio. The portfolio exercises are designed to assess the learning outcomes at a threshold level, that is, by completing the portfolio exercises you will demonstrate your knowledge, skills and behaviours at the minimum standard required to pass. To fully prepare for the terminal assessment you should complete all portfolio exercises and follow all additional guidance provided by a member of the teaching staff.

Each set of portfolio exercises are graded as Pass or Fail. You must pass all portfolio exercises to pass the portfolio.

Each worksheet has a due date. You are expected to complete the worksheet and submit the work before the indicated due date. If you do this then this is a good indication that you are working at the pace that we expect of you. Where you submit work after this due date, we will still provide feedback on it and it will still contribute towards your portfolio.

Once you submit your portfolio exercise, you will receive feedback from the graders. If your first submission does not pass, you will be allowed to resubmit after making changes. You are allowed to resubmit your portfolio exercise as many times as you need to achieve a pass, as long as those submissions are completed by the late submission deadline.

Terminal assessment

The terminal assessment is an assessment which takes place at the end of a teaching period.

Feedback approach

Feedback refers to the information provided to you about your performance on various academic tasks, such as assignments, exams, projects, or class participation. The purpose of feedback is to help you understand your strengths and areas for improvement, guiding your learning journey and enhancing your academic skills.

You will receive feedback on the formative activities on the weekly worksheets. Feedback will be provided in the tutorial or lab classes. During the lab sessions and tutorials you will be able to engage in dialogue with a teaching assistant to receive feedback. Some feedback may be written, depending on the nature of the activities.

You will be provided with an indication of performance on summative portfolio exercises. You can request feedback on your terminal assessment by contacting the module leader. You will not be able to request a copy of your marked terminal assessment.

Resit opportunity

At the University of Leeds you are ordinarily granted two attempts to pass a module. Your first attempt will allow you to achieve a grade between 0 and 100. Your second attempt is capped, allowing you to achieve a maximum grade of 40. If you have passed a module then you are not permitted to resit it.

Under some circumstances you may be eligible for a further attempt, or a first attempt resit. Eligibility is subject to an approved mitigating circumstances application. Information about mitigating circumstances can be found in the Mitigating circumstance guidance.

If you have not been awarded credits for the module then you will have an opportunity to resit the module. You must take this resit opportunity at the next available point in time that the assessment is offered. The next available opportunity is during the August resit period. You must register to take the resit. Information about how to register will be provided by email after you have received your results.

To resit the module you will be required to complete the portfolio to a satisfactory standard. The resit attempt will be capped at the pass grade 40. If you have applied for, and have been granted a first attempt resit then you will also have an opportunity to resit the terminal assessment for an uncapped grade.

Note you must submit and pass the portfolio in order to be awarded credit for this module.

6 Academic Integrity Academic integrity statement

Academic integrity is a commitment to good study practices and shared values, which ensures that your work is a true expression of your own understanding and ideas, giving credit to others where their work contributes to yours.

This University definition of academic integrity recognises that each individual has a respon sibility to contribute honestly within our academic community. Breaching academic integrity standards can lead to serious penalties. Make sure that you are familiar with the information in the pages below, complete the relevant Academic Integrity Tutorials and Tests, and develop your understanding of good study practices through the Skills@Library webpages to ensure that your own work is your best work. Learn more about our definition of academic integrity by exploring the headers and links below.

Declaration of academic integrity

When you submit for assessment, you are declaring to the University that your work is your own. By submitting work, you confirm that you have read and understood the University’s definition of Academic Integrity and the University’s rules on Academic Misconduct and are aware that if you commit an academic misconduct offence there can be serious disciplinary

consequences.

For more information about the University expectations of working with academic integrity and support available to you see the Library’s academic integrity webpage. If you have concerns, or would like support or guidance, please contact your personal tutor and/or your module tutor for advice.

For more information on academic misconduct offences, see the Academic Misconduct Proce dure.

8 Accessibility Accessibility statement

We have taken great care to avoid bias of any kind in the preparation of this module and related assessment materials. In compliance with the UK Equality Act (2010) we have designed this module to avoid any direct and indirect discrimination.

The standard assessment arrangements may present unnecessary barriers for candidates with disabilities or learning difficulties. Reasonable adjustments are available to students to enable them to access the assessments and receive recognition of their attainment. Reasonable adjust ments will only be agreed where they do not give students an unfair advantage over others or compromise the standards being assessed.

Students who cannot access the assessment of any component may be able to receive an award based on the parts of the assessment they have completed

You can request reasonable adjustments to learning resources by receiving a support summary sheet from the University of Leeds Disability Services. Your support summary sheet will be shared with the module leaders and other relevant teaching staff. You may wish to speak individually to the module leader to discuss how the adjustments will be implemented from your support summary sheet.

You can request reasonable adjustments to assessments by receiving a support summary sheet from the University of Leeds Disability Services. The University of Leeds will automatically implement any adjustments indicated on your support summary sheet for examination based assessments. Your support summary sheet will be shared with the module leader and other relevant teaching staff. You may wish to speak individually to the module leader to discuss how the adjustments will be implemented from your support summary sheet concerning assessments.
